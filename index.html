Real batik: wax is drawn with a canting (a pen) or pressed on with a copper stamp called a cap. Wax resists dye, so waxed areas keep their color. But wax cracks when the cloth is dipped or crumpled, and dye seeps through those hairline cracks. That crackle is the signature look of real batik. Artists dye, add more wax, and dye again, working from light to dark. At the end, the wax is boiled out.
